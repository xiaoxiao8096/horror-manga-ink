# Value Map and Line Grammar v2

Use this reference after identifying the source subject and scene. Apply the visual system in order; do not treat hatching, screentone, or texture as an all-over filter.

## Four-Layer Value System

1. **Solid black:** Reserve for hair masses, garment interiors, deep street/corridor recession, cast-shadow cores, and other large structural anchors.
2. **Paper white:** Preserve only a few focal highlights: eye/glasses reflections, lamp glow, wet-surface glints, or a contour edge that separates the face/hand from shadow.
3. **Halftone midtone:** Use grouped screentone fields to bridge black and white on skin, walls, sky, and distant space.
4. **Directional hatching:** Use only where form, light, or psychological density needs further explanation. Follow hair flow, cheek planes, fabric folds, road recession, cloud direction, or architectural planes.

Never replace the four-layer system with smooth gray airbrushing. Never cover every region with hatching or dots.

## Line Hierarchy

Use imperfect heavy outer contours only at high-contrast silhouette boundaries. Use finer interior lines for eyelids, nose bridge, lip edge, hair strands, glasses rims, folds, and spatial depth. Vary density by importance: the focal face/hand/object receives precise local detail; distant or emotionally quiet regions receive simplified fields or negative space.

## Pressure Through Restraint

Create psychological pressure through a close crop, an off-center practical light, a black field, corridor/street recession, a held expression, or a single visually loud white zone. Do not add characters, monsters, wounds, blood, body alteration, extra anatomy, or a new event.

## Negative Space and Grain

Keep blank or lightly toned paper fields adjacent to dense blacks. Use fine irregular paper grain only in broad empty regions or shadow transitions. Grain must never obscure the subject identity, glasses, labels, or a crucial object edge.

## Anti-Drift Block

Always suppress pastel/color accents, glossy oversized eyes, blush, smooth cel shading, clean vector fills, chibi proportions, cheerful enhancement, painterly brushes, airbrushed gray fog, neon rim light, high-gloss digital rendering, and generic scratch-noise overlays.

## Close Night Portrait Modifier

Preserve the source vertical ratio, close-camera perspective, face shape, glasses, hair silhouette, and street depth. Make hair and street recessions controlled black masses. Render glasses with crisp contour geometry and selective paper-white reflections. Translate a warm streetlamp into a compact paper-white glow with halftone falloff. Describe cheeks and under-eyes through sparse directional hatching; never smooth, reshape, or beautify the face.
