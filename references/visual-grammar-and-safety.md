# Visual Grammar and Safety

Use a descriptive visual grammar rather than naming a living artist, a copyrighted series, a comic panel, or a character. The target is a general Japanese black-and-white psychological-horror manga atmosphere.

## Visual grammar

| Element | Use | Do not use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink line | Varied contour weight, etched detail, irregular hand-inked energy | Color outlines, glossy vector lines, photorealistic render textures |
| Shadow | Pure-black masses with legible edge control | Muddy gray airbrush shading or accidental black crush over the subject |
| Hatching | Crosshatching and directional hatch strokes that follow form and light | Uniform scratch noise over every surface |
| Hatching-gray / screentone | Sparse local hatch clusters create gray where form turns; use a small isolated tone field only when the source requires it | Full-image dot blankets, gray wash, all-over screentone, or decorative dots that erase facial identity/text |
| Texture | Fine paper grain, non-figurative ink irregularity, restrained organic patterns | Gore, wounds, parasites, extra anatomy, or a new narrative event |
| Mood | Ordinary factual setting plus one existing unsettling tension cue, silence, and close observation | Generic dark filter, comedy, cute/chibi style, action-panel chaos, invented event, or direct imitation of a named creator |

## Fidelity lock

Preserve the source ratio, subject identity, face shape, expression, hairstyle, clothing, pose, body/hand count, camera angle, perspective, primary objects, and scene structure. Preserve all people in groups and their relative positions. Do not crop without instruction. If text exists, do not invent replacement text; note that stylization can make pre-existing text less readable.

## One-click decision

Use medium intensity by default. Ask a question only when the user explicitly requests extreme distortion, extra characters, body transformation, gore, readable dialogue, a copyrighted character, or a direct named-artist recreation. Otherwise transform once and deliver one result.

## Lightweight acceptance check

Confirm: the output is monochrome; the ordinary source setting remains readable; one existing detail—not a new event—carries the tension; the primary subject remains recognizable; source ratio remains intact; broad white space and source-supported black shapes are present; no unwanted new people/objects/text, gore, or body distortion appears; and the visual language is original black-ink psychological horror rather than a specific artist or existing work.
