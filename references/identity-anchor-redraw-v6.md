# Identity-Anchor Manga Redraw v6

Use this reference when the user wants a photo to become a unified original manga illustration rather than a photo sketch.

## Separate Identity from Surface

Preserve only hard identity and scene anchors: facial proportions, glasses shape, hairstyle silhouette, expression, clothing silhouette, source framing, lens viewpoint, street direction, building/vehicle/lamp placement, and overall setting. Redraw all soft photographic surface information: pores, lens blur, digital noise, individual hair strands, small reflection fragments, gradient shadows, incidental background edges, and color traces.

## Redraw, Do Not Trace

Rebuild the source into one coherent graphic language. Use crisp black outlines and fill shapes, selected gray texture made from controlled pen marks, and clean white paper. Simplify the face into a small number of clear planes; group hair into deliberate shape clusters; redraw street, vehicle, tree, lamp, pavement, and buildings as designed ink objects with consistent line weight. Do not preserve a visible photographic base beneath the ink.

## Character Continuity

Keep face shape, glasses, hair silhouette, eye spacing, nose/mouth relationship, neutral expression, and clothing identity recognizable. Do not trace every skin contour, retain pores, or idealize the face. The person must read as a drawn character belonging to the same graphic world as the environment.

## Scene Continuity

Keep the same location and composition, but choose only the source features required for recognition: building planes, pavement direction, one vehicle silhouette, tree mass, practical lamp, shutter/doorway, and dark recess. Remove incidental photographic clutter. Give every drawn object the same contour, black fill, gray texture, and white-space logic.

## Anti-Sketch Gate

Reject and regenerate if the output reads as a pencil sketch, charcoal sketch, photorealistic ink portrait, pen tracing of a photograph, crosshatching over every visible surface, or a photo with a line filter. The required outcome must read as an original unified black-and-white comic illustration at first glance.
