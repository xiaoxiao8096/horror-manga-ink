# Ordinary World and Single Tension v3

Read this before every transformation. Treat the result as an original black-ink psychological-horror still, not a generic comic filter.

## Core Scene Rule

Keep the source world **ordinary, factual, and readable**. Preserve the photo's normal street, room, weather, clothing, objects, and facial expression. Create unease by making one already-present detail carry disproportionate weight: a held gaze, a close camera distance, a single practical lamp, a narrow recess, a reflection, an empty corridor, an overly still hand, or a small anatomical/architectural detail observed too carefully. Do not add an event, a creature, an injury, a distortion, or a new person.

## Black Ink and White Paper

Use broad unmarked paper-white fields as active space. Use pure black only for source-supported structural shapes, such as hair, garment interiors, deep recesses, or cast-shadow cores. Create gray with sparse, precise hatching and short line clusters that follow real anatomy, fabric, architecture, or depth. Do not cover the full image with screentone, dot patterns, gray wash, or scratch texture.

## Detail Allocation

Select one or two anchors for close observation: eyes, glasses reflection, eyelid crease, lip line, hairline, a hand, a small wall detail, a nearby object edge, or a light source. Give these anchors fine variable-weight pen detail. Simplify quiet regions into white paper, clear black silhouette, or minimal contour. Unease comes from the imbalance between ordinary space and excessive attention to one normal detail.

## Portrait Rule

Preserve actual face shape, eye scale, eye spacing, gaze, mouth, hair, glasses, pose, and lens perspective. Keep most facial skin as white paper. Use fine hatching only under the eyes, at cheek planes, beneath the nose, around the lip line, and where the source light truly falls. Do not make eyes glossy, enormous, cute, symmetrical beyond the source, smooth, or beauty-retouched.

## Close Night Selfie Rule

Keep the original vertical ratio and near-camera framing. Use hair and dark clothing as main black shapes. Keep the practical lamp as a small, hard paper-white point; give it only a narrow ink halo. Keep the street recognizable and mostly simple. Treat glasses as crisp, thin geometry with selective white reflection. Let the unchanged gaze and close framing be the single tension cue; do not invent paranormal content.

## Prohibitions

Do not use all-over halftone, all-over hatching, gray airbrush, generic film grain, decorative ink splatter, plain black background replacement, painterly lighting, neon rim light, color, cute anime drift, beauty illustration, or comic-panel text. Do not mimic a named living creator, a copyrighted character, a published panel, or a specific comic.
